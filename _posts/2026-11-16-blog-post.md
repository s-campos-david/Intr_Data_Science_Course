What does a hospital with poor maternal outcomes look like? I looked at US hospital data from CMS to find out. CMS groups scored hospitals into Top, Middle or Bottom Performers based on severe birth complications. Among the 2,360 hospitals with a label, about one in four is a Bottom Performer.

Here is what stood out to me.

## The state makes a big difference in the picture

Among states with at least 25 scored hospitals, the share of Bottom Performers goes from about 8% in Kansas to 51% in New York.

I expected the story to be mostly about individual hospitals. That gap made me think more about what hospitals share: local health systems, access to care and state policies. It also raises a question worth asking: how much of the difference comes from care, and how much comes from how complications are recorded?

The map gives us a place to start asking better questions, not a reason to blame a state.

## Higher cesarean rates came with worse labels more often

Across five groups, from the lowest to the highest cesarean rates, the share of Bottom Performers rises steadily from 21.5% to 32.5%.

That makes cesarean rates worth a closer look when discussing hospital outcomes. It does not show that cesareans caused the complications, but it is a clear pattern in this comparison.

## The safety statement told me little about the result

For SM_7, the maternal safety attestation, 25.1% of hospitals reporting "Yes" were Bottom Performers. Among other responses, the share was 24.7%. Almost no difference.

Those are shares of hospitals in the Bottom Performer group, not patient complication rates. The lesson for me is simple: reporting safety practices is not the same as showing better outcomes. This comparison says little about whether the practices themselves work.

## A higher score is not always a better answer

A model that says no hospital is a Bottom Performer gets 74.9% of predictions right. But it finds none of the hospitals I was looking for.

My logistic regression model gets fewer predictions right overall, 60.3%, but finds about half of the Bottom Performers. Only one in three flags is correct, so it is still far from reliable.

This changed how I read the results. The goal was to find hospitals that might need a closer look, not just collect correct answers from the larger group.

## What I take from this

The state differences deserve attention. Cesarean rates show a pattern worth exploring. The safety statement alone tells us little about the label. And a model can look good on accuracy while missing the whole point.

I would look next for information about staffing, patients and the communities these hospitals serve. The results are a starting point for more questions, not a guide for choosing care or judging a hospital. They come from one test split and need checking on new data.
